How development financing is impacting land values

Carson BrantleyAlpha Capital Realty

Back in 2021, construction financing was around 4.5–5.5%, and lenders were covering roughly 65–75% of a project's cost. Today, construction loans are closer to 8–9%, and lenders are generally covering less of the total project.

To put that into perspective, on a $10 million construction loan over a two-year build, the interest cost alone could be roughly $500K back in 2021 versus $850K today. That's an additional $350K in financing costs before even factoring in the other expenses of developing a property.

What that means for landowners is that developers have less room to pay for a site. They work backward from what the finished building will be worth, subtract construction costs, financing, and their required profit, and what's left determines what they can pay for the land.

That's a big part of what's influencing development pricing today. Even when a property has strong development potential, the numbers have to make sense for a buyer to move forward.

Rates are approximate as of November 2026 and for illustration only. The interest example assumes the loan is drawn evenly over a 24-month build, so about half is outstanding on average, at 5% (2021) and 8.5% (today).
